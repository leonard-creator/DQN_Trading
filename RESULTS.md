### What this means for the project

1. **Buy-and-hold is the hardest baseline at realistic costs.** To pass H1 the agent needs a median net Sharpe clearly above ~0.67 on the training universe, and the margin must hold up under a permutation test with Holm correction over 50 (seed, fold) pairs.
2. **Costs decide whether active rules survive.** At ~15–20× annual turnover, each 10 bp of cost removes roughly 0.25–0.4 Sharpe (momentum 0.54 → 0.28, MACD 0.83 → 0.44 from 0 to 10 bp). An agent that trades as often as the random agent (38×/yr) loses ~4 % CAGR to costs at 10 bp. Turnover will be a first-class diagnostic in M2.
3. **The regime matters more than the rule.** Per-fold medians move by more than 1.5 Sharpe between folds for the same strategy, e.g. buy-and-hold goes from 1.45 in F2 to −0.21 in F5. A result from one fold, like all past single-split runs in this repo, says little.
4. **The random agent has positive Sharpe without costs (0.45)**, purely from ~50 % long exposure to markets that mostly rose. This is the effect that made the old € validation numbers look good (`docs/CODEBASE_NOTES.md` §5).
5. **^GDAXI on its own is a hard single-asset test case.** No active baseline beats a 0.39 buy-and-hold Sharpe there at 10 bp. This is the M2 reference asset.

### Overfitting statistics

Not applicable for M1. The deflated Sharpe and PBO are computed over **agent** configurations; baselines are not counted as trials (PROTOCOL §10a.4). Both functions are implemented and tested (`tests/test_stats.py`).

### Stability (the M2 go criterion)

| Config | Best checkpoint at (median) | Late drift of inner-val Sharpe (median; runs < −0.5) | Mean-Q range | Seed IQR within a fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | 31 % of training | −0.15; 6/50 | −0.1 … 0.3 | 0.15 |
| m2_dqn_base | 31 % | −0.13; 10/50 | 0.5 … 12 | 0.30 |
| ablations | 26 – 31 % | −0.12 … −0.29; 10–17/50 | 0.5 … 13 | 0.33 – 0.41 |

Late drift = mean inner-validation Sharpe in the last quarter of training minus the second quarter. The best-checkpoint comparison is not used, because the maximum of ~20 noisy one-year Sharpe estimates overstates any drop (see the full report).

### What this means

1. **No catastrophic collapse.** Q-values stay bounded and no configuration falls apart late in training. There is a **mild, consistent decline after ~30 % of training**. It appears with the legacy hard target + constant LR **and** with the new soft target + LR decay. So it is not a target-drift or learning-rate effect but **overfitting to the training period**, the plan's alternative explanation (plan §1). This supports the owner's decision to halve the training budget for M3 (100k transitions).
2. **The Phase-2 fixes did not improve outer-validation performance; legacy is better.**
   - The new agents trade **4× more** (≈ 30× vs 7× capital per year) and are long less often (60 % vs 73 %).
   - On a mostly rising index, being long was the best strategy. Even without costs they reach only 0.26–0.31, below buy-and-hold (0.39). So the new agents add trading **without** timing skill, and costs then remove another ~0.2 Sharpe.
   - Legacy's profit-at-sell reward ignores costs and does not punish holding. That makes it a near-buy-and-hold policy (holding period 87 bars).
3. **Dueling, n-step and PER change nothing measurable** (all within ±0.05 of base, well inside the seed noise). This matches Guijarro-Ordonez et al. (2026): refinements on the allocation side add little; signal extraction is what matters (plan §7).
4. **Seed variation is large:** a typical within-fold IQR of 0.3–0.4 Sharpe, the same size as the differences between configurations. Single-seed comparisons would be misleading.
5. **For H1:** on the single asset, no configuration beats buy-and-hold net of costs. The fixes are kept for M3/M4 (they make training well-defined and stable), but **the reward and the input signal, not the network refinements, are where M3/M4 must make progress.** The best-supported next step is a reward that charges for turnover where it matters, measured relative to holding.

## M3 — Reward variants on ^GDAXI (2026-10-05)

Four reward designs, each fixed **before** its results were seen and each changing one thing against the reference `m3_dqn_base_100k` (diff_sharpe, 100k transitions). Every config is 10 seeds × 5 folds = 50 runs; trials 9–12 of 50. Generated by `python scripts/analyze_m2.py --milestone M3`; full tables: [`experiments/reports/M3_rewards.md`](experiments/reports/M3_rewards.md).

| Config | Reward |
|---|---|
| `m3_dqn_base_100k` | reference: differential Sharpe ratio (`diff_sharpe`) |
| `m3_mean_variance` | x − (λ/2)x², x = net return / ex-ante daily vol, λ = 0.5 (plan Phase B) |
| `m3_vol_scaled_pnl` | net return / ex-ante daily vol (Zhang, Zohren & Roberts 2019) |
| `m3_active_return` | (net return − buy-and-hold return) / ex-ante daily vol (new: rewards only what H1 tests) |
| `m3_diff_sharpe_shadow3` | reference, but in training trades look 3× as expensive (new: against M2's overtrading) |

### Performance on ^GDAXI (c = 10 bp, median (IQR) over 50 runs)

| Config | Sharpe | Sharpe at 0 bp | Turnover / yr | Exposure | Avg hold (bars) | Δ vs buy-and-hold (Holm p) | vs momentum (Holm p) | Deflated Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| m3_dqn_base_100k | 0.17 (0.50) | 0.39 | 29.7 | 59 % | 21 | −0.19 (1.0) | +0.05 (0.47) | 0.44 |
| m3_mean_variance | −0.01 (0.82) | 0.27 | 30.8 | 38 % | 9 | −0.36 (1.0) | −0.12 (1.0) | 0.37 |
| **m3_vol_scaled_pnl** | **0.21** (0.61) | 0.39 | 28.7 | 70 % | 33 | −0.12 (1.0) | **+0.12 (0.045)** | 0.59 |
| m3_active_return | 0.13 (0.59) | 0.32 | 27.9 | 68 % | 27 | −0.19 (1.0) | +0.05 (0.34) | 0.53 |
| m3_diff_sharpe_shadow3 | 0.17 (0.68) | 0.34 | **22.2** | 67 % | **43** | −0.14 (1.0) | +0.11 (0.10) | 0.58 |
| *buy-and-hold* | *0.39* (0.32) | *0.39* | *0.5* | *100 %* | *505* | | | |

N_trials = 12. **PBO over the 5 M3 configurations: 0.59.** The training is stable in all five: late drift −0.14 … +0.13, no collapse, Q bounded.

### What this means

1. **The M3 kill criterion (plan §5) is not met.** No reward beats buy-and-hold on median validation Sharpe net of costs. The best is `vol_scaled_pnl` at 0.21 vs 0.39.
2. **Before costs, the agents now match buy-and-hold; they do not beat it.** At 0 bp the reference and `vol_scaled_pnl` reach 0.39, the same as buy-and-hold, while holding only 59–70 % on average. The whole gap at 10 bp is trading cost (≈ 29× capital turned over per year). This is an improvement on M2, where the agents reached only 0.26–0.31 at 0 bp, and it comes from the halved training budget.
3. **The ranking among rewards is not reliable (PBO 0.59 > 0.5).** In most CSCV splits the in-sample best reward is at or below the median out of sample. `vol_scaled_pnl` is the best median, the lowest seed spread (within-fold IQR 0.29) and the only significant win over momentum. But its lead over the reference (+0.04 median) is noise-sized.
4. **`mean_variance` with λ = 0.5 in volatility units is far too risk-averse.** The variance penalty (0.046 per step) is larger than the whole reward (0.032), so the agent stays mostly flat (38 % exposure, 9-bar holds) and misses the rising market. λ was the plan's Phase-F start value, but in daily-vol units a comparable penalty would be λ ≈ 0.05. That would be a new trial.
5. **The shadow cost works mechanically but does not create skill.** Turnover −25 %, holding period ×2, more robust at 25 bp (−0.07 vs −0.17), same Sharpe at 10 bp. Even ×3 leaves 22 turns per year, far from buy-and-hold's 0.5.
6. **`active_return` adds no timing skill** (0.13). It is the only variant still improving at the end of training (inner Sharpe 0.95 → 1.11, best checkpoint at 42 %).

**Conclusion for H1:** on a single broad index, reward design closes the *gross* gap to buy-and-hold but not the *net* gap. The agents' decisions earn about what the market earns, and every extra trade is a cost. With M1 (no timing baseline wins) and M2 (network refinements do not matter), this points to the **input signal and the data** (M4, plan §7 Track A) as the remaining levers.

### What this means

1. **The M4 go criterion is met, clearly, by the conv-transformer.** Trained across 26 ETFs, it does better on 7 ETFs it never saw than the same agent trained on ^GDAXI alone (+0.27 Sharpe, p < 0.001). Pooling assets generalises, as Sirignano & Cont (2018) report for stocks. With the conv encoder the advantage is small and not significant.
2. **The encoder matters more than anything tried before.** The same data, features and reward give 0.16 with the conv encoder and 0.44 with the conv-transformer. This matches Guijarro-Ordonez, Pelger & Zanotti (2026): signal extraction is the separating element, while allocation refinements (M2) and reward shapes (M3) moved little.
3. **But the gain is not timing skill.** The transformer trades half as much (16 vs 31.5 turns/yr) and stays invested more (84 % vs 62 %, a median holding period of 365 trading days ≈ 1.5 years). A constant long position has the same Sharpe as buy-and-hold (0.67) at any exposure, and the transformer stays below that **even at 0 bp** (0.58). Its improvement comes from overtrading less and being long more, i.e. it moves *towards* buy-and-hold. It now beats momentum significantly (Holm p = 0.011) and roughly ties MACD, but its timing still subtracts value relative to staying invested.
4. **The residual features add nothing measurable** (0.16 vs 0.18). They only reduce seed spread. Broad ETFs carry little idiosyncratic signal, as anticipated in plan §7.1.
5. **More data makes training more robust.** All cross-asset runs show late drift ≈ 0 and no collapses, and their best checkpoints come later (37–47 % of training vs ≈ 30 % single-asset).
6. **For H1:** no configuration beats buy-and-hold net of costs on any evaluation set. The best deflated Sharpe is 0.69 (bar: 0.95).

**Provenance note (added 2026-10-06): close-time leak in the ^GDAXI sleeve.** The residual features (`resid`, `resid_cum30`) of ^GDAXI subtract factor returns built from the US ETFs' *same-day* returns. Those settle at 16:00 New York time, 4.5 hours after the DAX close at which the decision is taken (PROTOCOL Part II §V2.3, Q1). This affects ^GDAXI in `m4_cross_resid`, `m4_cross_resid_transformer` and `m4_single_resid`; `m4_cross_base` (no residual features) and all baselines are unaffected. The numbers above stay as reported. No conclusion changes: the affected ^GDAXI results were weak (0.03–0.18), and ^GDAXI is 1 of 26 sleeves of the H1 set. The leak is fixed in v2 Step 0b, and v2 compares against the clean re-run R0′ (Step 0d). *Quantified in Step 0b:* the leaked feature had a timing IC of −0.15 with the next-day DAX return, yet the ^GDAXI sleeve of all three affected trials shows a timing IC of −0.007 to +0.007, the same as `m4_cross_base` without the feature (+0.010). The agents did not use the leak, so it did not inflate any number above.

## Secondary: neo-broker cost scenario (2026-10-05, owner request)

**Not part of H1.** H1 is evaluated only at 10 bp + 1 bp (PROTOCOL §7, §10a.9). Full tables: [`experiments/reports/cost_scenarios.md`](experiments/reports/cost_scenarios.md), produced by `python scripts/rescore_costs.py`.

**Scenario** (`config/cost_scenarios.yaml`; rough estimates, not verified quotes):
- EUR 1 for **every transaction**: each buy and each sell, so a round trip costs EUR 2.
- EUR 10,000 capital.
- 3 bp half-spread.
- 15 bp/yr TER on held exposure for ^GDAXI only. ETF prices already have their TER deducted, so charging it there would double-count.
- Variants: EUR 1k / 50k capital, 1 / 5 bp half-spread.

- *Re-scored:* the exposures of finished trials, measured under the scenario. Same strategies, so these are **not** new trials.

The search of PROTOCOL Part II §V12.1 item 23 (`harness/hpo.py`), for the owner's objective of timing on a 1–4-week horizon at small costs:
- 32 learner settings (a balanced random design), all with V1b's decision structure, **no buy-and-hold head start** (anchor 0, no prior) and 5 bp per unit traded.
- Three worlds: W-null (no timing exists), W-regime (bull/bear) and the new **W-swing** (each ticker's own trend; the oracle holds about 3–4 weeks and trades 17 times a year).
- *Oracle share* = the agent's median gain over buy-and-hold ÷ the oracle's median gain. Score = mean oracle share over W-swing and W-regime, −1 if the agent loses more than 0.05 in W-null (noise trading).
- Successive halving: 32 settings at ¼ of the training budget (seeds 0–4) → 8 at ½ (seeds 0–9) → 2 at the full budget on **new** seeds 10–19.
- Synthetic only, **no trials** (N_trials stays 19). Full tables: [`experiments/reports/V2_hpo.md`](experiments/reports/V2_hpo.md).

#### What this means

1. **AHL-Gaussian does not improve our agent.**
   - The learnt range ends near our fixed one: fat-tailed targets keep pulling it wider. It times exactly as well (c00: 70 % / 36 % with either range).
   - But for c00 it trades noise in W-null (16 turns/yr, −0.09), probably because its bins keep moving during training.
   - The fixed, data-based range stays. This matches the prior expectation: the paper fixes value scales that are unknown and growing, which ours are not.
2. **A narrow range (AHL-paper) sharpens the value differences, which cuts both ways.** It captures more swing timing (+0.04 to +0.10 Sharpe on matched worlds) but also trades more noise in W-null (7–8 of 10 worlds lose). It is a capture-vs-noise trade-off, not a free gain.
3. **c00 with the fixed range holds up on new seeds:**
   - It is the only configuration that is clean in W-null (median −0.00, 2 turns/yr, 3 of 10 worlds losing).
   - It captures 70 % of the oracle in W-swing; in W-regime 36 % here vs 60 % on seeds 10–19, which is seed variance.
   - It is the most robust setting across all four seed sets (rounds 1–3 and this screen).
4. **c19 trades noise at the full budget in every variant** (W-null −0.13 to −0.19), so it drops out.
5. **c25 sits exactly on the noise limit with every range** (W-null −0.047 to −0.053). Its scores flip between +0.68 and −0.40 on that threshold, so its "AHL win" is an artefact of the cut-off, not an effect of AHL.

#### Improvement ideas

- *Inside the plan:* keep the fixed range. Carry **c00** to real data first.
- *Needs an owner decision:* the narrow-range effect (more capture, more noise) suggests pairing a narrower range with a stricter gate (z 1.5–2). That could keep the extra capture without the noise trades; it is a cheap synthetic screen.
- *Method note:* the hard −0.05 cut-off makes borderline settings flip. For future screens, report the W-null loss continuously next to the score, as done here.

1. **The synthetic timing skill does not transfer to the real ETFs.**
   - The agents trade as designed (17–32 turns a year, 2–4-week holds) but find no edge: timing IC ≈ 0.005, and even before costs they trail buy-and-hold (0.54–0.64 vs 0.67).

2. **The likely reason is a mismatch between W-swing and the data.**

- *Needs an owner decision (no trial):* a **realism check**. - *Inside the protocol:* the V3 signal pack (cross-asset and slower inputs, e.g. bond → equity momentum) is the one untested bundle that adds information rather than learning capacity.
- *Needs an owner decision:* c00's learner with the buy-and-hold head start back on ("deviate only when sure"). It probably reverts to V4's behaviour, i.e. it is safe but adds nothing.

   - A rule estimated on past data trades on the owner's horizon (17-day holds) but loses to buy-and-hold **even before costs** (0.51 vs 0.67) and in 4 of 5 folds.
   - The reversion is too weak and too unstable (strong in crises and in 2013–19, weak in 2020–23) to pay for being out of rising markets 37 % of the time.
3. **Together with M1** (momentum and MACD below buy-and-hold) and the H trials, this is consistent evidence: **1–4-week timing of these broad ETFs is not supported by the data at realistic costs.** In both directions, trend and reversal, the timing signal is smaller than the drift lost by stepping aside plus the costs.
4. **Different asset classes behave differently** (commodities trend, equities and bonds revert). One shared policy has to average over opposite patterns.

### Improvement ideas

- *Needs a trial:* an asset-class input (equity / bond / commodity / real estate), so one network can learn class-specific timing, such as momentum in commodities and reversion in equities. The data shows the patterns differ in sign.
- *Objective (owner decision):* since 1–4-week timing does not pay at these costs, more promising goals are **risk control** (H3, the drawdown hypothesis: lower drawdown at the same Sharpe) or **cheaper execution** (fewer, larger decisions; the weekly-decision variant 3w).
- *Data (owner decision, own protocol):* where the short-horizon structure is stronger, e.g. the relative (market-neutral) residuals of Track B / H2, which revert by construction. M4 found the residual features weak as *inputs* for direction timing, but they have not been tested as the *target* of a long-short strategy.
