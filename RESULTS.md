# Results

Results under [`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md) (v1.2), one section per milestone. Every number here comes from a run logged in `experiments/trials.csv`. Full tables are in `experiments/reports/`.

**Reading the numbers.** Each cell is the **median (IQR)** over 10 seeds × 5 walk-forward folds (validation 2014-01 → 2023-09). Each (seed, fold) value is computed on an equal-weight portfolio of the evaluation tickers, net of costs. Sharpe is annualised with rf = 0. **The frozen test period (2023-10 → 2026-09) has not been touched.**

## M1 — Baselines (2026-10-05)

Generated by `python scripts/run_baselines.py --descriptive`. Full tables (all cost levels and evaluation sets): [`experiments/reports/M1_baselines.md`](experiments/reports/M1_baselines.md).

### Go criterion: reproducibility ✅

Each baseline was run twice. All metrics of all 7 baseline configurations were **bit-identical** on the re-run, including the seeded random agent. The input data are frozen in `data/raw/` with sha256 hashes in `MANIFEST.json`.

### The bar the agent has to clear (training universe, 26 tickers, c = 10 bp)

### What this means for the project

1. **Buy-and-hold is the hardest baseline at realistic costs.** To pass H1 the agent needs a median net Sharpe clearly above ~0.67 on the training universe, and the margin must hold up under a permutation test with Holm correction over 50 (seed, fold) pairs.
2. **Costs decide whether active rules survive.** At ~15–20× annual turnover, each 10 bp of cost removes roughly 0.25–0.4 Sharpe (momentum 0.54 → 0.28, MACD 0.83 → 0.44 from 0 to 10 bp). An agent that trades as often as the random agent (38×/yr) loses ~4 % CAGR to costs at 10 bp. Turnover will be a first-class diagnostic in M2.
3. **The regime matters more than the rule.** Per-fold medians move by more than 1.5 Sharpe between folds for the same strategy, e.g. buy-and-hold goes from 1.45 in F2 to −0.21 in F5. A result from one fold, like all past single-split runs in this repo, says little.
4. **The random agent has positive Sharpe without costs (0.45)**, purely from ~50 % long exposure to markets that mostly rose. This is the effect that made the old € validation numbers look good (`docs/CODEBASE_NOTES.md` §5).
5. **^GDAXI on its own is a hard single-asset test case.** No active baseline beats a 0.39 buy-and-hold Sharpe there at 10 bp. This is the M2 reference asset.

### Overfitting statistics

Not applicable for M1. The deflated Sharpe and PBO are computed over **agent** configurations; baselines are not counted as trials (PROTOCOL §10a.4). Both functions are implemented and tested (`tests/test_stats.py`).

## M2 — Model fixes on the single asset ^GDAXI (2026-10-05)

Five configurations, all fixed **before** any agent result was seen, each 10 seeds × 5 folds = 50 training runs. Generated by `python scripts/analyze_m2.py`; full tables: [`experiments/reports/M2_agent.md`](experiments/reports/M2_agent.md).

| Config | What it is |
|---|---|
| `legacy` | the old algorithmic choices: MSE, GAP pooling, profit-at-sell reward, no action masking, whole-series episodes, hard target copy, constant LR 1e-3 |
| `m2_dqn_base` | the Phase-2 fixes: Flatten, Huber on normalised `diff_sharpe` rewards, Double DQN with masking, cosine LR, soft target τ = 0.005, one-year random-start episodes |
| `_dueling`, `_nstep`, `_per` | base + one change each: dueling head / 3-step returns / prioritised replay |

| m2_dqn_dueling | 0.05 (0.42) | −0.1 % | 18.6 % | 29.8 | 61 % |
| m2_dqn_nstep | 0.05 (0.68) | −0.4 % | 19.6 % | 26.6 | 59 % |
| m2_dqn_per | 0.05 (0.51) | −0.6 % | 18.7 % | 30.9 | 57 % |
| *buy-and-hold* | *0.39* (0.32) | *6.0 %* | *23.8 %* | *0.5* | *100 %* |

### Statistics (c = 10 bp, 50 (seed, fold) pairs, Holm over the 4 baselines)

| Config | Δ Sharpe vs buy-and-hold (p) | vs momentum | vs MACD | vs random | Deflated Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | +0.06 (p = 0.10) | +0.30 (p < 0.001) | +0.56 (p < 0.001) | +0.52 (p < 0.001) | 0.72 |
| m2_dqn_base | −0.27 (p = 1.0) | −0.03 (p = 1.0) | +0.22 (p = 0.001) | +0.18 (p = 0.007) | 0.46 |
| ablations | −0.23 … −0.28 | −0.04 … +0.01 | +0.22 … +0.27 | +0.18 … +0.22 | 0.43 – 0.51 |

**PBO over the 5 configurations: 0.02.** Picking the in-sample best (legacy) is not overfitting; it is consistently best. N_trials = 5. The deflated Sharpe ratios are all below the 0.95 bar.

### Stability (the M2 go criterion)

| Config | Best checkpoint at (median) | Late drift of inner-val Sharpe (median; runs < −0.5) | Mean-Q range | Seed IQR within a fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | 31 % of training | −0.15; 6/50 | −0.1 … 0.3 | 0.15 |
| m2_dqn_base | 31 % | −0.13; 10/50 | 0.5 … 12 | 0.30 |
| ablations | 26 – 31 % | −0.12 … −0.29; 10–17/50 | 0.5 … 13 | 0.33 – 0.41 |

Late drift = mean inner-validation Sharpe in the last quarter of training minus the second quarter. The best-checkpoint comparison is not used, because the maximum of ~20 noisy one-year Sharpe estimates overstates any drop (see the full report).

### What this means

1. **No catastrophic collapse.** Q-values stay bounded and no configuration falls apart late in training. There is a **mild, consistent decline after ~30 % of training**. It appears with the legacy hard target + constant LR **and** with the new soft target + LR decay. So it is not a target-drift or learning-rate effect but **overfitting to the training period**, the plan's alternative explanation (plan §1). This supports the owner's decision to halve the training budget for M3 (100k transitions).
2. **The Phase-2 fixes did not improve outer-validation performance; legacy is better.**
   - The new agents trade **4× more** (≈ 30× vs 7× capital per year) and are long less often (60 % vs 73 %).
   - On a mostly rising index, being long was the best strategy. Even without costs they reach only 0.26–0.31, below buy-and-hold (0.39). So the new agents add trading **without** timing skill, and costs then remove another ~0.2 Sharpe.
   - Legacy's profit-at-sell reward ignores costs and does not punish holding. That makes it a near-buy-and-hold policy (holding period 87 bars).
3. **Dueling, n-step and PER change nothing measurable** (all within ±0.05 of base, well inside the seed noise). This matches Guijarro-Ordonez et al. (2026): refinements on the allocation side add little; signal extraction is what matters (plan §7).
4. **Seed variation is large:** a typical within-fold IQR of 0.3–0.4 Sharpe, the same size as the differences between configurations. Single-seed comparisons would be misleading.
5. **For H1:** on the single asset, no configuration beats buy-and-hold net of costs. The fixes are kept for M3/M4 (they make training well-defined and stable), but **the reward and the input signal, not the network refinements, are where M3/M4 must make progress.** The best-supported next step is a reward that charges for turnover where it matters, measured relative to holding.

### Provenance notes

- The `code_hash` of these five trials was computed when each row was written, and partly includes `scripts/`. ## Secondary: neo-broker cost scenario (2026-10-05, owner request)

**Not part of H1.** H1 is evaluated only at 10 bp + 1 bp (PROTOCOL §7, §10a.9). Full tables: [`experiments/reports/cost_scenarios.md`](experiments/reports/cost_scenarios.md), produced by `python scripts/rescore_costs.py`.

**Scenario** (`config/cost_scenarios.yaml`; rough estimates, not verified quotes):
- EUR 1 for **every transaction**: each buy and each sell, so a round trip costs EUR 2.
- EUR 10,000 capital.
- 3 bp half-spread.
- 15 bp/yr TER on held exposure for ^GDAXI only. ETF prices already have their TER deducted, so charging it there would double-count.
- Variants: EUR 1k / 50k capital, 1 / 5 bp half-spread.

**Two kinds of numbers:**
- *Re-scored:* the exposures of finished trials, measured under the scenario. Same strategies, so these are **not** new trials.
- *Trained under the scenario:* `nb_dqn_base` (= `m3_dqn_base_100k` but the €1 fee and spread are in its reward and checkpoint score) and `nb_dqn_k2` (the same with larger steps, exposure 0 / 50 / 100 %). These are trials 7 and 8 of 50.

### Single asset ^GDAXI, median Sharpe over 10 seeds × 5 folds

| Strategy | 10 bp (H1) | Neo-broker EUR 10k | EUR 1k | EUR 50k | Transactions / yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | 0.33 | 0.34 | 0.13 | 0.35 | 28 |
| m2_dqn_base | 0.09 | 0.18 | −0.64 | 0.24 | 124 |
| m3_dqn_base_100k | 0.17 | **0.26** | −0.55 | 0.33 | 119 |
| nb_dqn_base † | 0.03 | 0.13 | −0.70 | 0.22 | 122 |
| nb_dqn_k2 † | −0.06 | 0.17 | −0.60 | 0.24 | 107 |
| *buy-and-hold* | *0.39* | *0.38* | *0.38* | *0.38* | *0.5* |
| *momentum* | *0.07* | *0.15* | *0.03* | *0.16* | *13.5* |

† trained under neo-broker costs. The dueling, n-step and PER agents score 0.11–0.14 at EUR 10k (see the report).

### Against the baselines under the neo-broker scenario (50 (seed, fold) pairs, Holm over 4 baselines)

| Agent | Δ Sharpe vs buy-and-hold (Holm p) | vs momentum | Deflated Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|
| legacy | +0.09 (p = 0.033) | +0.26 (p < 0.001) | 0.72 |
| m3_dqn_base_100k | −0.10 (p = 0.98) | +0.06 (p = 0.32) | 0.57 |
| nb_dqn_base † | −0.16 (p = 1.0) | +0.00 (p = 0.98) | 0.52 |
| nb_dqn_k2 † | −0.20 (p = 1.0) | −0.03 (p = 1.0) | 0.57 |

N_trials = 8. No deflated Sharpe reaches 0.95.

### What this means

1. **For one ETF on EUR 10k, the flat fee is cheaper than the 10 bp assumption.** Every active agent gains about +0.1 Sharpe when re-scored. Buy-and-hold is unaffected (one transaction per two years).
2. **Training under the cheaper costs did not create trading skill.**
   - `nb_dqn_base` trades as often as its 10 bp twin (≈ 120 transactions / yr). Under its own scenario it scores 0.13, vs 0.26 for the 10 bp-trained reference.
   - The gap is inside the seed noise (IQR ≈ 0.5), so the correct reading is "no improvement", not "worse".
   - Larger steps (K = 2) do not help either (0.17).
   - The agent has no timing edge to exploit. Cheaper trades only make its existing trading less costly. This matches M2: without costs the agents also stay below buy-and-hold.
3. **Capital decides everything for a flat fee.**
   - At EUR 1k, ≈ 120 transactions cost ≈ 12 % a year and every active agent is clearly negative.
   - For a 26-ETF portfolio on EUR 10k, one ¼ step costs ≈ 104 bp, and momentum, MACD and random collapse (report §2).
   - The cross-asset agent of M4 is therefore only realistic with a broker fee at much larger capital (≥ ~EUR 100k), or with far fewer transactions.
4. **Legacy beats buy-and-hold on the *mean* pairwise difference (+0.09, Holm p = 0.033), but this is not a robust result.**
   - Its *median* Sharpe (0.34) is below buy-and-hold (0.38).
   - Its deflated Sharpe (0.72) is far below 0.95.
   - It comes from a secondary scenario chosen after M2 had been seen.
   - Legacy is a near-buy-and-hold policy (73 % exposure, 87-day holds). A slightly positive difference most likely reflects avoiding a few drawdowns by chance, not timing skill. It is recorded here for completeness, not as evidence for H1.
5. **Next steps are unchanged:** the reward and the input signal (M3/M4, plan §7 Track A), not the cost model or the network refinements.
